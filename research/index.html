UNDERGRADUATE THESIS · MANUSCRIPT SUBMITTED · 2025–2026

CAAM-Net: Conflict-Aware Multimodal Bangla Clickbait Detection

In multimodal clickbait detection, headlines and their associated thumbnails can convey conflicting signals. My undergraduate thesis investigates these inconsistencies in Bangla news and develops a conflict-aware model combining language and visual representations.

Method

  • Integrated BanglaBERT and ConvNeXtV2-Tiny with bidirectional cross-attention and conflict-aware prediction.
  • Created synthetic training conflicts through opposite-class thumbnail substitution.
  • Prepared 8,108 validated title–thumbnail pairs and evaluated against 11 baselines using channel-disjoint testing and ablation studies.

Results

The model achieved mean Macro-F1 of 0.7641 under standard evaluation and 0.7512 under synthetic conflict evaluation. These results exceeded the strongest conventional multimodal baseline by 4.45 and 15.58 percentage points, respectively.

A manuscript based on this undergraduate thesis has been submitted and has passed technical checks. As of the 20 October 2026 status update, editorial assignment was pending. It is not yet accepted or published, and no public manuscript link is available.

PUBLISHED RESEARCH · 2025

Bangla Hate Speech Detection: Transformer Ensembles & Multi-task Learning

This work investigates fine-grained Bangla hate speech detection across type, target, and severity. It explores transformer-based ensembles and a shared-encoder multi-task learning setup with subtask-specific heads.

Contributions

  • Developed multilingual transformer-based classification approaches for Bangla hate speech.
  • Designed task-specific ensembles across transformer models and training seeds, alongside a shared-encoder multi-task architecture.
  • Performed comparative experiments and error analysis of code-mixed slang, implicit hate, and ambiguous targets.

Publication

Nahid Hasan. “Catalyst at BLP-2025 Task 1: Transformer Ensembles and Multi-task Learning Approaches for Bangla Hate Speech Detection.” Proceedings of the Second Workshop on Bangla Language Processing, pp. 468–475, 2025. Association for Computational Linguistics.
